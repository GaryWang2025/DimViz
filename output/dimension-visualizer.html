ℝ¹ · One-Dimensional Space

Space Definition
A one-dimensional vector space over the real numbers ℝ, the set of all real numbers.
$$\mathbb{R}^1 = \{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$$
Basis
The unique basis vector $\mathbf{e}_1$; any vector is a scalar multiple of it.
$$\mathbf{e}_1 = (1) \quad \dim(\mathbb{R}^1) = 1$$
$$\mathbf{x} = x_1 \mathbf{e}_1, \quad x_1 \in \mathbb{R}$$
Norm
A measure of vector length satisfying positivity, homogeneity, and the triangle inequality.
$$\|\mathbf{x}\| = |x_1|$$
Linear Map $T: \mathbb{R}^1 \to \mathbb{R}^1$
$$T(x) = ax, \quad a \in \mathbb{R}$$

ℝ² · Two-Dimensional Space

Space Definition
The set of all ordered pairs of real numbers with addition and scalar multiplication.
$$\mathbb{R}^2 = \{(x_1, x_2) \mid x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}\}$$
Standard Orthonormal Basis
$$\mathbf{e}_1 = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathbf{e}_2 = \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
$$\mathbf{x} = x_1\mathbf{e}_1 + x_2\mathbf{e}_2, \quad \dim(\mathbb{R}^2)=2$$
Euclidean Norm
$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2}$$
Linear Transform $A: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$
$$A = \begin{pmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix},\quad \det(A) = ad - bc$$
Rotation Matrix
$$R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$$

ℝ³ · Three-Dimensional Space

Space Definition
$$\mathbb{R}^3 = \{(x_1,x_2,x_3) \mid x_i \in \mathbb{R}\}$$
Standard Orthonormal Basis
$$\mathbf{e}_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\ \mathbf{e}_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\ \mathbf{e}_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
$$\mathbf{v} = v_1\mathbf{e}_1 + v_2\mathbf{e}_2 + v_3\mathbf{e}_3$$
Euclidean Norm
$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2}$$
Inner Product (Dot Product)
$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} u_i v_i = \|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|\cos\theta$$
Cross Product
$$\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = \det\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{e}_1 & \mathbf{e}_2 & \mathbf{e}_3 \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3\end{pmatrix}$$
Eigenvalue Equation
$$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v} \implies \det(A - \lambda I) = 0$$
Unit Sphere
$$S^2 = \{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^3 \mid \|\mathbf{v}\|=1\}$$

ℝ⁴ · Four-Dimensional Space

Space Definition
Ordered 4-tuples of independent real coordinates. Cannot be directly perceived visually; understood through projection and analogy.
$$\mathbb{R}^4 = \{(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) \mid x_i \in \mathbb{R}\}$$
Standard Orthonormal Basis
$$\mathbf{e}_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{e}_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{e}_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{e}_4=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
$$\dim(\mathbb{R}^4) = 4$$
Euclidean Norm
$$\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 + v_4^2}$$
Inner Product
$$\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \sum_{i=1}^{4} u_i v_i$$
4D Rotation Matrix SO(4)
4D rotation occurs in planes (e.g. $xy, xw, yw$). There are 6 independent rotation planes.
$$R_{xw}(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & 0 & 0 & -\sin\theta \\ 0&1&0&0 \\ 0&0&1&0 \\ \sin\theta & 0 & 0 & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$$
Hypercube (Tesseract)
The 4D analog of a cube: 8 cubic cells, 24 square faces, 32 edges, and 16 vertices.
$$\text{Vertices: } (\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1) \quad (16 \text{ total})$$
Unit Hypersphere $S^3$
The set of points in ℝ⁴ at distance 1 from the origin (a 3-dimensional hypersphere).
$$S^3 = \{\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^4 \mid \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1\}$$
$$\text{Hypervolume}(S^3) = 2\pi^2$$
